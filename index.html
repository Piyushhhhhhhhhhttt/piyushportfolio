Composing visual narratives in digital space. Blending brutalist minimalism, Swiss editorial grids, and kinetic motion.

Operating from Kathmandu under the moniker ¥U5H, I work across graphic design, visual identity systems, album packaging, and interactive web direction. Every layout is approached like a musical score—balancing stark structural silence with sudden visual chords.

Each image is a single note; each project is a complete composition. Whether designing tactile print monographs, multi-layered typographic posters, or 60fps GSAP web experiences, the goal remains raw clarity without decorative noise.

2026.10 / Note 01

Sampling & Swiss Grids: Why Every Layout Needs a Backbeat

Chopped drum breaks from 1970s soul records and Josef Müller-Brockmann's mathematical poster grids share the exact same foundation: subdividing space into strict intervals so that a single off-grid element hits with maximum tension.

When we strip away rounded buttons, drop shadows, and decorative badges, the rhythm of raw proportion and negative space carries the entire emotional weight of the interface.

2026.08 / Note 02

Chiaroscuro & High-Contrast Display Serifs

Pairing hair-thin serif strokes at 200px scale against 10px technical monospace creates an immediate architectural scale jump—eliminating the need for ornamental UI chrome.

The handwritten pencil layer acts as the human margin note on top of the printed score, reminding the viewer that behind the rigid grid is an individual designer's hand.
